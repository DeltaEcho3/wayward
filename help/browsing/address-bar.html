Search or go to an address

How-to

Use the address bar to search the web or enter a website address. It floats over the page, at the bottom of the screen unless you moved it to the top.

What the bar shows

  • Back and Forward arrows at its ends. An arrow fades when there's nowhere to go.
  • A lock or a globe before the site's name. The lock means the connection is secure (TalkBack reads it as Secure connection). The globe means it isn't (Not secure): the page came over plain http, or you chose to open a site whose certificate couldn't be checked.
  • The site's name. Its own name (like example.com) is in full ink and the rest is softer. A name too long for the bar is cut at its start with a fade, so the part that tells you whose site it is always shows.
  • The round shield button (TalkBack: Shields), with a badge counting what was blocked on the page. See what the shield shows.
  • On the start page there's no site yet, so the bar reads Search or type an address.
  • In a space that keeps its own sign-ins, a dot in the space's color sits between the arrows. A locked space shows a small lock in its color instead. See spaces.
  • On a locked tab the bar only says Locked tab. Tapping it asks for your fingerprint, face or PIN instead of opening the editor.

Search or type an address

  1. Tap the address bar. The keyboard comes up and the address is selected, so whatever you type replaces it. On your search engine's results page it starts with your search words instead, so you can change them.
  2. Type some words, or an address like example.com.
  3. Pick a suggestion, or press Go on the keyboard.

To leave without going anywhere, tap the X at the end of the field (TalkBack: Cancel), or press Back.

The suggestions

While you type, the field keeps the bar's look, with the same thin edge and lettering. A tray of suggestions opens next to it, with the best match nearest the field. The rows keep their place while you type, so the one you're about to tap doesn't jump away.

  • The first row is what you typed, marked Search. For an address it's marked Go to this address instead.
  • Open tabs that match say "Switch to tab · example.com" (with their own site), and take you to that tab instead of opening the page again.
  • Bookmarks have a star and pages from your history have a clock.
  • Rows marked Search suggestion come from your search engine. You can turn them off in Settings > Search > Search suggestions. They never show in private tabs.
  • While you type words, a Search with card lists your other search engines. Tap one to run just this search there.

What counts as an address: a name with a dot and an ending made of letters (example.com) opens over https. Number addresses like 192.168.1.1 and localhost open too, which is handy for a router. Anything with a space in it, and a single word like "router", is searched. To open a one-word name on your own network, type http:// in front of it.

Nothing you type leaves your phone until you pick something, apart from the search suggestions, which send what you've typed to your search engine.

With the Minimalist Address Bar

If you use the Minimalist Address Bar (Settings > Customize > Minimalist Address Bar), the address bar is one floating pill with no arrows. Its field shows the site's name, or a search icon and Search or Address on the start page, with the assistant's stars at its end when the assistant is on. Tap it and the whole pill becomes the field, with the suggestions above it.
