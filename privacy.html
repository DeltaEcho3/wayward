Wayward Privacy Policy

Last updated: November 12, 2026.

The short version

Wayward does not send us your browsing history, the websites you visit, the videos or files you download, your cookies, or the contents of web pages. We don't run analytics, ads or tracking in the app. Feedback and support reports are optional, and you see everything in one before you send it. The AI assistant is different: when you use it, the content you choose to share is sent to the AI provider you picked.

Who we are

Wayward is made by Donald Evans III, doing business as Delta Echo Three. Contact: trywayward@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

  • Tabs. Tabs keep their cookies, site data and history (the pages you visit in them) on your phone, in Wayward's private storage, until you clear browsing data. Your history and your bookmarks are kept encrypted, each with a key of its own held in Android's keystore. When this version first starts, they move out of the unencrypted file earlier versions kept, as soon as the keystore can be reached; until then nothing new is saved in that file, though deleting from it still works. Clearing your history, Emergency delete and deleting it whenever Wayward closes destroy history's key before its file. Shredding a site or clearing history from a time range moves what's left to a new key. Deleting a single page, or the oldest pages going once history is full, overwrites them inside the file. Cookies, site data and the engine's cache aren't encrypted by Wayward itself; Android's own encryption covers them, as it does all of Wayward's storage. Open tabs are saved so they reopen next time: their addresses, Back history, scroll position, form entries, a small picture of each page, whether you pinned one and the name you gave it, and their groups (each group's name, color and whether it's pinned), encrypted with a key kept in Android's keystore. You can turn history off, or have Wayward delete all of this whenever it closes.
  • Private tabs. A private tab keeps no history and isn't saved, and its cookies and site data are wiped when the last private tab closes, when Wayward is closed, or when you clear browsing data. Bookmarks and start page shortcuts you add from one are kept, as are its downloads. You can make private tabs the default.
  • Spaces. Each space's name, color and switches are kept on your phone, encrypted with a key from Android's keystore. A space set to keep its sign-ins separate keeps its sites' cookies, site data, cache, sign-ins and permissions apart from your other spaces, until you clear or delete it (or clear browsing data, which reaches every space). A file you download by its address from such a space (Download link, saving an image, a video file the browser engine fetches when the downloader is refused) is fetched without cookies. A settings backup includes each space's name, color and switches, never its tabs or the sites in it.
  • Emergency delete. In the Tabs screen's menu (and on its bar, if you put it there), it closes every tab and deletes all of the above at once, after asking. Bookmarks and downloads stay.
  • The start page. What a new tab and Home show is drawn by Wayward itself and loads nothing from the internet: your most visited sites (from your history, as letters; sites you remove are remembered on your phone), shortcuts you add yourself (their names and addresses, kept on your phone without any password, tracking tags or sign-in parts of the address, until you remove them; clearing browsing data keeps them, while shredding a site deletes its shortcuts, and Emergency delete and "Delete everything when Wayward closes" delete them all; a page from a private tab can be added, and is kept like any other, as a bookmark made in one is; a locked tab's page can't be added; a private tab's start page shows these, never the most visited), your other open tabs, and how many ads and trackers uBlock Origin blocked this week. That count is kept on your phone as one number per day, never from private tabs. Tapping it shows the count by day and the host names uBlock Origin blocked most, kept on your phone for 14 days. They're mostly trackers' and ad servers' names, like an ad network's domain, but a site's own host names can be among them (its stats or ad addresses), so they're never kept from private or locked tabs, nor while history is off. Clearing your history, deleting a site's last page from history or shredding the site, Delete everything and "Delete everything when Wayward closes" delete them; nothing about them leaves your phone. You can have new tabs open on your search engine's own page instead (Settings > Tabs & history). With Site icons on (Settings > Customize, on unless you turn it off), Wayward asks a site on your start page for its icon when you visit it, with a few requests to that site, without cookies and never from a private or locked tab. The icons stay on your phone, encrypted, and go when you clear your history or that part of it, remove the site from the start page, or turn the option off.
  • Home page wallpaper. A picture you choose for the home page comes through Android's own picker, so Wayward can't see your other photos. A locked-down helper on your phone reads it once (for a HEIC photo, Android's own media service does), and Wayward keeps its own copy in private storage, without the picture's location or other details. It isn't sent anywhere and isn't part of a settings backup. Removing the wallpaper deletes the copy; your original is never changed.
  • Camera and microphone. Only when a site asks and you tap Allow (Wayward asks every time), and only for that page while it's open. The video and sound go to that site, not to us.
  • QR scanner. Scan QR code (tap the address bar) uses the camera only while the scanner is open, and Android asks you before the first time. Each picture is read on your phone for a code (with ZXing) and then dropped: none is saved or sent anywhere. What the code says is shown to you first; nothing opens or is searched until you tap Open or Search, and details like a Wi-Fi password or a contact card can only be copied.
  • Site notifications. Only from a site you allow (Wayward asks each site once; Settings > Site permissions lists them), only while Wayward is running, and never from private tabs. Your phone shows them; nothing goes to us.
  • Protected video. When a streaming site plays DRM-protected video and you allow it, your phone's own DRM system (Widevine) gets a license from that site's license server. Nothing goes to us. Turn it off in Settings > Media.
  • Dark mode for websites. Darkening a site (Settings > Accessibility, Darken sites without a dark look) happens on your phone, with Dark Reader. To work out a page's colors it may read the page's own style sheets again, from where the page got them. Nothing goes to us or to Dark Reader.
  • Reader view. Finding a page's article and showing it as a plain reading page (Menu > Reader view) happens on your phone, with Readability. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Page translation. Translating a page (Menu > Translate page, or the offer under a page) happens on your phone, with Mozilla's translator; the page's text isn't sent anywhere. The translator and each language's files download from Mozilla the first time they're needed and stay on your phone. Which languages sites see you read is set in Settings > Languages.
  • Links to other apps. When a page's link wants to open another app on your phone (like Gmail), Wayward asks first, every time. If you tap Open, that app gets the link; nothing goes to us.
  • Downloads. Saved inside the app by default, behind your fingerprint, face or PIN if you keep the downloads lock on. If you choose a download folder, files are saved there instead, where other apps on your phone can see them; a private tab's downloads always stay inside the app. Moving a file out of the app, sharing files kept inside it with another app, saving a copy to your gallery or phone, or opening a private tab's file in another app asks you first, unless you turn that off. A paused download keeps the part it has so far inside the app, with the address it last came from and how the site identified that version of the file, so Resume can ask for just the rest. These go when the download finishes or you delete it. From 1.4.3, a private tab's go when its private browsing ends, or when you pause it after that, and Resume then starts it over. One still downloading then (waiting for a connection or to try again included) goes on without cookies, which went with private browsing, and keeps them while it does; if it stops before finishing, they go at the latest when private browsing next ends or Wayward next starts. Deleting a download also deletes what Wayward kept about it (its address, title, file name and the space it came from). With Settings > Downloads > Delete saved files after, files inside the app are deleted once they're that old.
  • Settings, your AI keys and your Premium status. Stored on your phone; each key (OpenRouter, Anthropic, OpenAI or xAI, whichever you add) is encrypted with Android's keystore.
  • Saved tasks and receipts. A task you save for the AI assistant (its name and what to ask) is kept on your phone, encrypted with a key from Android's keystore, until you delete it; its words go to your AI provider only when you run it. If you add a task to your home screen, your phone's launcher keeps its name (not what it asks) for the icon, outside Wayward's encryption, until you remove the icon or delete the task. Emergency delete keeps saved tasks, as it keeps bookmarks. A receipt (what one message to the assistant did: what you asked, the sites and searches it used, what went to your AI provider, your approvals, and the names of videos it saved, shown only while Downloads is unlocked) stays only while Wayward is open; it's never saved, and leaves your phone only if you share it yourself.
  • The assistant's chat. Kept on your phone, encrypted with a key from Android's keystore, so it's still there when you come back: what you and the assistant said, the page text it read in your last few messages, and the names of the sites it read in that chat (these stay after the older text goes, so it can ask before typing or pressing on a different site), never pictures or the names of saved videos. It's deleted by New chat, emergency delete, clearing your history, turning the assistant off, changing its provider or Premium ending. You can also delete the list of sites by itself, from the chat's menu. A chat that used a locked tab comes back locked, and one that holds a saved file's transcript locks whenever Downloads is locked. If you tap Save chat, a copy goes to the place you pick, as an encrypted file or a text file. Wayward doesn't send it anywhere else, but if you pick a cloud service, that service gets it under its own terms. The encrypted file is locked with a password you choose, which Wayward doesn't keep, so it can't be recovered if you lose it, and anyone holding the file could keep guessing at a weak one. The text file isn't encrypted. Either copy stays until you delete it. A chat that used a locked tab or locked Downloads can't be saved this way. Opening an encrypted file shows it to you on your phone and nowhere else: it isn't sent to the assistant or kept.

We have no servers that receive any of this.

The AI assistant

The assistant is off until you turn it on, and it uses your own account with the AI provider you pick: OpenRouter, Anthropic, OpenAI or xAI (for Grok). Before it reads a page, Wayward asks you, shows the site and the AI provider, and lets you see exactly what would be sent. If you say no, nothing from that page is sent. With Compare tabs, the pages of the tabs you pick (their address, title and text) go together after one card that names their sites; Wayward never sends the AI a list of your tabs. Text you share or select in another app and send to Wayward only goes into the assistant's message box, until you send it. You can skip that card if you want: tick "Always share this site" on it, turn on "Always share pages" in the assistant's settings, or let it act on its own ("Always allow", which reads pages without the card too; a task you OK'd at its start does the same for that task). Each is explained before it takes effect, and the settings let you undo them.

What's sent from a page is its address, title, text, links and buttons, and a video's captions if the assistant reads them. When a video has no captions, the assistant can turn its speech into text on your phone, with your phone's own speech recognizer; the audio stays on the phone, and only the text goes to your AI provider, shared the same way as the page's text. You can also ask about a video or audio file saved inside Wayward (never a private tab's): its speech is turned into text on your phone the same way, and only that text goes, with your question, after you tap Share; not the file or its name. With a model that can see pictures, the assistant can also take screenshots of the page on your screen. Before a screenshot leaves your phone, Wayward blacks out every form field and anything typed in one, and frames from other sites unless the sharing card named them. Wayward's own buttons and panels are never in it. The assistant never uses private tabs.

A few things are sent without that card, because the assistant needs them to know where it is: the results page when it searches DuckDuckGo for you, and the address and title of a page after it opens one, follows a link or goes back. When the assistant opens a page or searches, that site can see which site you were on (its domain only), the same as when you follow a link. Your AI provider (and, through OpenRouter, the model's maker) processes your requests under its own privacy policy and retention practices, which may differ from ours. Wayward asks OpenAI and xAI not to keep requests for later turns; their own retention still applies. With xAI, Wayward also sends a random id for each conversation, so xAI can bill the parts it has seen before at its cheaper cached price; the id contains nothing about you and is new for each conversation. Don't use the assistant on pages with personal or sensitive information.

Feedback and support reports

Feedback (Settings > Support > Feedback) is off until you create it, and nothing is sent automatically: you see the whole report before you send it. An idea contains only the type and area you choose, the app version and build, the browser engine version, your Android major version and any description you type. A report about a problem contains the same, and also:

  • your phone's maker and model (never its serial number or any other ID), and the versions of Wayward's downloader (yt-dlp), its media sniffer and uBlock Origin;
  • a short error code for the last failure, with a rough time (like "today");
  • the settings that change how Wayward behaves: where the address bar is and how many rows it has, Full screen pages, whether all Shields are on, HTTPS-only, Secure DNS (off, or its provider's name; "a custom provider" for your own, never its address), how many spaces you have and whether private tabs are locked, Wayward's language (the one you picked, or your phone's), the assistant's provider and model (never your key; a model you typed in only if it looks like a model's name), and whether downloads stay inside Wayward or go to a folder on your phone (not which one);
  • a log of the last 50 things Wayward did that explain a failure, each in a few words with a code and how long ago, like "download ended DL_EXPIRED" or "assistant: provider answered 429": how a download, a video's lookup or yt-dlp's daily update went, the AI provider refusing a message or the assistant failing, a page's process crashing, the media sniffer starting (each time Wayward starts) or it or uBlock Origin not starting, and the app crashing. Never an address, a page's text or title, a file name, a chat or a key. Of a private tab's, only how a download or lookup ended, or that its page's process stopped, marked as private;
  • for "The app crashed", the last crash: when (roughly), in which version, the kind of error and its message, and where in Wayward's code and its libraries it happened. Addresses, file paths, email addresses and long codes are taken out of the message, and it's left out entirely if a private tab was open.

Wayward keeps the last crash and the log on your phone so a report can include them, even if you never send one, in its own storage, which isn't backed up. A new crash replaces the last one, and the log keeps only its last 50 lines. Clearing browsing data, Emergency delete and "Delete everything when Wayward closes" delete both.

Links and file paths are removed from your description, but anything else you type is sent as written. You send the report yourself through your phone's share sheet, usually by email. That email service and our mailbox then hold the message. We keep a report only as long as we need it to answer you or fix the problem, and delete it sooner if you ask: write to trywayward@gmail.com.

Purchases

Premium is bought through Google Play. Google processes the payment and tells Wayward whether you own Premium. We don't receive your payment details.

Children

Wayward is not directed at children under 13.

Your rights

Wayward doesn't collect personal information, so usually we hold nothing about you. If you sent us a report or an email, you can ask what we have and ask us to delete it: write to trywayward@gmail.com. We don't sell or share personal information.

Changes
