Wayward Privacy Policy

Last updated: October 30, 2026.

The short version

Wayward does not send us your browsing history, the websites you visit, the videos or files you download, your cookies, or the contents of web pages. We don't run analytics, ads or tracking in the app. Feedback and support reports are optional, and you see everything in one before you send it. The AI assistant is different: when you use it, the content you choose to share is sent to the AI provider you picked.

Who we are

Wayward is made by Donald Evans III, doing business as Delta Echo Three. Contact: trywayward@gmail.com.

What stays on your phone

  • Tabs. Tabs keep their cookies, site data and history (the pages you visit in them) on your phone, in Wayward's private storage, until you clear browsing data. Open tabs are saved so they reopen next time: their addresses, Back history, scroll position, form entries and a small picture of each page, encrypted with a key kept in Android's keystore. You can turn history off, or have Wayward delete all of this whenever it closes.
  • Private tabs. A private tab keeps no history and isn't saved, and its cookies and site data are wiped when the last private tab closes, when Wayward is closed, or when you clear browsing data. You can make private tabs the default.
  • Emergency delete. The red button on the Tabs screen closes every tab and deletes all of the above at once.
  • The start page. What a new tab and Home show is drawn by Wayward itself and loads nothing from the internet: your most visited sites (from your history, as letters, with no icons fetched; sites you remove are remembered on your phone), your other open tabs, and how many ads and trackers uBlock Origin blocked this week. That count is kept on your phone as one number per day, never which sites, and never from private tabs. You can have new tabs open on your search engine's own page instead (Settings > Tabs & history).
  • Camera and microphone. Only when a site asks and you tap Allow (Wayward asks every time), and only for that page while it's open. The video and sound go to that site, not to us.
  • QR scanner. Scan QR code (tap the address bar) uses the camera only while the scanner is open, and Android asks you before the first time. Each picture is read on your phone for a code (with ZXing) and then dropped: none is saved or sent anywhere. What the code says is shown to you first; nothing opens or is searched until you tap Open or Search, and details like a Wi-Fi password or a contact card can only be copied.
  • Site notifications. Only from a site you allow (Wayward asks each site once; Settings > Site permissions lists them), only while Wayward is running, and never from private tabs. Your phone shows them; nothing goes to us.
  • Protected video. When a streaming site plays DRM-protected video and you allow it, your phone's own DRM system (Widevine) gets a license from that site's license server. Nothing goes to us. Turn it off in Settings > Media.
  • Dark mode for websites. Darkening a site (Settings > Accessibility, Darken sites without a dark look) happens on your phone, with Dark Reader. To work out a page's colors it may read the page's own style sheets again, from where the page got them. Nothing goes to us or to Dark Reader.
  • Reader view. Finding a page's article and showing it as a plain reading page (Menu > Reader view) happens on your phone, with Readability. Nothing is sent anywhere.
  • Page translation. Translating a page (Menu > Translate page, or the offer under a page) happens on your phone, with Mozilla's translator; the page's text isn't sent anywhere. The translator and each language's files download from Mozilla the first time they're needed and stay on your phone. Which languages sites see you read is set in Settings > Languages.
  • Links to other apps. When a page's link wants to open another app on your phone (like Gmail), Wayward asks first, every time. If you tap Open, that app gets the link; nothing goes to us.
  • Downloads. Saved inside the app by default, behind your fingerprint, face or PIN if you keep the downloads lock on. If you choose a download folder, files are saved there instead, where other apps on your phone can see them.
  • Settings, your AI keys and your Premium status. Stored on your phone; each key (OpenRouter, Anthropic or OpenAI, whichever you add) is encrypted with Android's keystore.

We have no servers that receive any of this.

The AI assistant

The assistant is off until you turn it on, and it uses your own account with the AI provider you pick: OpenRouter, Anthropic or OpenAI. Before it reads a page, Wayward asks you, shows the site and the AI provider, and lets you see exactly what would be sent. If you say no, nothing from that page is sent. You can skip that card if you want: tick "Always share this site" on it, turn on "Always share pages" in the assistant's settings, or let it act on its own ("Always allow", which reads pages without the card too; a task you OK'd at its start does the same for that task). Each is explained before it takes effect, and the settings let you undo them.

What's sent from a page is its address, title, text, links and buttons, and a video's captions if the assistant reads them. When a video has no captions, the assistant can turn its speech into text on your phone, with your phone's own speech recognizer; the audio stays on the phone, and only the text goes to your AI provider, shared the same way as the page's text. With a model that can see pictures, the assistant can also take screenshots of the page on your screen. Before a screenshot leaves your phone, Wayward blacks out every form field and anything typed in one, and frames from other sites unless the sharing card named them. Wayward's own buttons and panels are never in it. The assistant never uses private tabs.

A few things are sent without that card, because the assistant needs them to know where it is: the results page when it searches DuckDuckGo for you, and the address and title of a page after it opens one, follows a link or goes back. When the assistant opens a page or searches, that site can see which site you were on (its domain only), the same as when you follow a link. Your AI provider (and, through OpenRouter, the model's maker) processes your requests under its own privacy policy and retention practices, which may differ from ours. Wayward asks OpenAI not to keep requests for later turns; OpenAI's own retention still applies. Don't use the assistant on pages with personal or sensitive information.

Feedback and support reports

Feedback (Settings > Support > Feedback) is off until you create it, and nothing is sent automatically. A report about a problem, or an idea you send, contains only: the type and area you choose, the app version and build, the browser engine version, your Android major version, for a problem a short error code with a rough time (like "today"), and any description you type. Links and file paths are removed from your description, but anything else you type is sent as written. You send the report yourself through your phone's share sheet, usually by email. That email service and our mailbox then hold the message. We keep a report only as long as we need it to answer you or fix the problem, and delete it sooner if you ask: write to trywayward@gmail.com.

Purchases

Premium is bought through Google Play. Google processes the payment and tells Wayward whether you own Premium. We don't receive your payment details.

Children

Wayward is not directed at children under 13.

Your rights

Wayward doesn't collect personal information, so usually we hold nothing about you. If you sent us a report or an email, you can ask what we have and ask us to delete it: write to trywayward@gmail.com. We don't sell or share personal information.
